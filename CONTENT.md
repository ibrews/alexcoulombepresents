- `[data.products.colocation.name]` ▸ Co-Location Engine
- `[data.products.colocation.tagline]` ▸ Fiducial-marker-based co-location for Unreal Engine VR — real and virtual players sharing one room, without a shared coordinate system to begin with.
- `[data.products.colocation.pitch]` ▸ Multiplayer XR in a physical space has a chicken-and-egg problem: every headset needs to agree on where the room actually is, and nothing hands that to you for free. - `[data.products.colocation.sections.0.heading]` ▸ One rigid solve, not a position-then-yaw hack
- `[data.products.colocation.sections.0.body]` ▸ Position and rotation are solved jointly — a weighted planar rigid-body fit (Kabsch/Umeyama) across every visible marker at once, not a separate 2-marker yaw formula bolted onto an averaged position. More markers in view means a tighter, self-correcting solve instead of diminishing returns from just picking two.
- `[data.products.colocation.sections.1.heading]` ▸ Corrections you can't feel
- `[data.products.colocation.sections.1.body]` ▸ A locked-in position doesn't get nudged on every noisy frame. Drift correction is velocity-gated — standing still means never moving the player, walking gets an imperceptible pull toward true, and being grossly wrong (inches, sustained across multiple readings) triggers a full snap instead of a slow, visible slide. The goal is co-location that fades into the background.
- `[data.products.colocation.sections.2.heading]` ▸ Three independent detector families
- `[data.products.colocation.sections.2.body]` ▸ ArUco, AprilTag, and QR all feed the same localizer, each carrying its own reserved ID range so a printed marker map can mix families without collision. A digital-twin overlay renders the exact expected marker pattern at its known position, so misalignment is visible and diagnosable in headset — no guesswork.

- `[data.products.colocation.sections.3.body]` ▸ A three-tier headless harness validates the whole pipeline before it ever touches hardware: pure-math unit tests against synthetic marker geometry, a real render-target camera detecting a live 3D scene, and a fully scripted OpenXR device driving the packaged app end-to-end.
