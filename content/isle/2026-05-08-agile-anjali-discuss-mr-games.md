## Notes

- Origin: ~1.5 years iterating on the concept at HBS, drawing on a background in immersive theater. Core interest: the intersection of digital and physical experience -- "embodied games"
- Vision: a venue where people play through a story like a live-action video game, combining physical, mental, and mixed-reality challenges, with real player progression rather than isolated scoring
- Compared explicitly to Sandbox VR and Level99 (40 individually-scored minigames, no meta-progression) and Dream Park -- Isle's differentiator is relying on built space for a robust, story-driven throughline
- Early prototype attempt (two-player co-located MR, "awakening a forest clearing through noise") didn't work -- led to realizing the need for clearer technical boundaries between feasible mechanics and open-ended R&D
- First technical approach: room-based re-anchoring instead of persistent global tracking, for cost efficiency; floor-anchored props are far easier to track than free-grab objects given current headset limits
- Planned initial venue at the time: up to ~4,000 sqft, ~50 people, parties re-anchoring per room rather than sharing persistent digital assets across the whole space
- Business approach: Agile Lens prefers time-based billing over milestone contracts for R&D-heavy work like this
